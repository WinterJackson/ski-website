Why Traditional YouTube Ad Blockers Keep Breaking (And Why Native Automation Wins)

Why Ski automates a native button instead of intercepting network traffic.

A constantly moving target

YouTube changes its interface, advertising flow, and delivery mechanisms over time. Extensions built around fixed network rules or one markup selector can lose their assumptions when those systems change. That creates a cycle of breakage, filter updates, and another change to the player.

The invasive path

Traditional ad blockers intercept network requests and resources. On a video site, playback, captions, theater mode, and advertising state all need to stay consistent. Some blocking approaches encounter anti-adblock warnings or player conflicts. Those trade-offs are different from automating a visible control.

The native path

Ski waits for YouTube’s native Skip Ad button and activates it. It does not intercept the stream, block requests, or replace the player. Press Tab during an ad, or enable auto-skip for hands-free operation. The extension automates an action the interface already offers.

Less interference, clear limits

Ski cannot make an unavailable skip button appear. An unskippable ad remains unskippable, and timed controls still need to become available. Native automation reduces the systems Ski needs to alter, but no extension can predict every future platform change.

Local and open

The script is less than 22 KB. Detection and execution happen on your device, with zero telemetry or browsing-history collection. Its MIT-licensed source is available at github.com/WinterJackson/ski. Review the implementation, report a regression, or contribute a fix.

Resilience through evidence

Ski’s eight-signal engine combines text, geometry, context, and semantics instead of relying on one permanent selector. That smaller scope and layered detection offer a different engineering trade-off: precise assistance rather than broad interception.
